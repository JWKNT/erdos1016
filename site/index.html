A graph on n vertices is pancyclic if it has a cycle of every length from 3 to n. Erdős problem 1016 asks for the least number of edges of such a graph. The answer is n+log2⁡n+log∗⁡n, with an error of a bounded number of edges.

The tour has ten stops. Each stop gives one idea of the proof and a link to its place in the paper.

1 Pancyclic graphs

A cycle through all n vertices has n edges. A pancyclic graph needs more edges for the cycles of the shorter lengths.

A cycle is a closed path, and its length is its number of edges. Write n+h(n) for the least number of edges of a pancyclic graph on n vertices.

A graph on six vertices

Theorem 1.1 the minimum excess of a pancyclic graph

There is a constant A such that, for every n≥3,

log2⁡n+log∗⁡n−A≤h(n)≤⌊log2⁡n⌋+log∗⁡n+1.

Stop 4 gives the definition of log∗⁡n.

2 A count of cycles

A graph with few edges has few cycles, and a pancyclic graph needs n−2 cycles of different lengths.

An even subgraph is a set of edges that meets every vertex an even number of times. The rank of a connected graph is its number of edges minus its number of vertices plus one. A graph of rank r has exactly 2r even subgraphs. Each cycle is one of them, and the empty set is not a cycle.

A pancyclic graph with n+h edges has the rank h+1. Hence

n−2≤2h+1−1,soh≥log2⁡(n−1)−1.

Bondy stated this bound in 1971. It gives the term log2⁡n. It does not give the term log∗⁡n, because many cycles can have the same length.

3 Binary shortcuts

With k more edges, one graph can contain 2k consecutive cycle lengths.

Take a path on a long cycle, and divide it into k segments with 2,3,5,9,… edges. Add a shortcut to each segment: one edge between its two ends. A path can pass each segment along its edges or along its shortcut. The shortcuts make the path shorter by 1,2,4,8,… edges. Each number from 0 to 2k−1 is a sum of different powers of two. So these paths have 2k consecutive lengths.

Choose the shortcuts

Two ways close these paths to cycles. The first way is one edge between the two ends of the path. The second way is the rest of the long cycle. With k about log2⁡n, the two ways give the cycle lengths from k+1 to n, with at most one exception. One more edge gives that length.

4 The short lengths

A few more edges give the short cycles. Their number is the iterated logarithm.

The lengths from 3 to k are still necessary. An edge from the start of the path to the end of segment ⌈log2⁡k⌉ closes the first segments. It gives the lengths from about log2⁡k to k. The next edge closes the first ⌈log2⁡⌈log2⁡k⌉⌉ segments. This continues down to the length 3.

The iterated logarithm log∗⁡n is the number of times that log2⁡ must be applied to n until the result is at most 1. In addition to the long cycle, the number of edges of the construction is at most

⌊log2⁡n⌋+log∗⁡n+1.

This is the upper bound of the theorem.

5 The density of cycle lengths

The lower bound uses a number Φ that measures how well a graph of a given rank can fill an interval of cycle lengths.

Let a graph of rank r contain a cycle of each length from 3 to L. Its density is (L−2)/2r: the number of these lengths, divided by the number of even subgraphs. Let Φ(R) be the least upper bound of the densities of all graphs of rank at least R. The count of cycles gives Φ(R)≤1.

A pancyclic graph on n vertices of rank r has n−2≤Φ(r)2r. So a small value of Φ(r) means that the rank r=h+1 is large.

In the paper Section 3, page 3

6 A small subgraph with the short cycles

A graph with many cycle lengths has a small subgraph W that already contains a cycle of each short length.

Start with the empty subgraph. Add a cycle of the least length that the subgraph does not contain. Do this again until the rank of the subgraph is at least 2R.

A subgraph of rank s≥R contains at most Φ(R)2s consecutive cycle lengths from 3 on, by the definition of Φ. So each added cycle is short. The result W is a union of at most 2R cycles, and it has at most Φ(R)22R+2R+5R edges.

7 Outside parts

Remove the edges of W from a cycle. The rest gives the cycle, or the rest is a forest.

The outside part of an even subgraph is the set of its edges that are not in W. A forest is a set of edges without a cycle. Let the graph have the rank r, and let W have the rank t and m edges. Then the even subgraphs have 2r−t different outside parts.

Look at a cycle C. If its outside part contains a cycle, then that cycle is C. So this outside part belongs to one cycle length. If its outside part is a forest, then C has between 1 and m edges in W. So this outside part belongs to at most m cycle lengths.

Let p be the proportion of the outside parts that are forests. Then the number of different cycle lengths is at most

2r−t(pm+1−p).

8 Half of the outside parts

At most about half of the outside parts are forests. So the density decreases by a factor of almost two.

The subgraph W is a union of few cycles with few edges. So the forest estimate gives p≤12+20/log⁡log⁡log⁡r. Put this bound and the size of W into the count of stop 7. For every large R the result is

9 A tower of scales

Each scale of a tower of exponentials almost halves the density, and about log∗⁡r scales are below r.

Take the scales Rj+1=2256Rj. The errors of all steps have a finite sum. So Φ(Rj)≤c2−j for a constant c. This gives Φ(r)≤c′2−log∗⁡r for all r, with a second constant c′.

A pancyclic graph has n−2≤Φ(r)2r and r=h+1. Take logarithms. The result is the lower bound h(n)≥log2⁡n+log∗⁡n−A.
