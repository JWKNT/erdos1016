The theorem states that P_st/2^beta is either one or at most 15/16.
It concerns full saturation, not the open second-order excess above one half.

CLASSIFICATION
For subcubic graphs with degree-two terminals, every affine terminal join is
one terminal path together with vertex-disjoint cycles.
Thus saturation excludes a cycle disjoint from any terminal path.
The bridge-shore degree count is integral and positive.
Every bridge consequently lies on the terminal route.
A terminal cannot itself touch a bridge in a saturated graph.
Otherwise, every terminal path immediately crosses that bridge and avoids
a cycle on its starting shore.
The same obstruction excludes coincident attachment vertices inside a piece.
These observations justify the two-terminal normalization of every piece.

In a bridgeless piece, two internally disjoint terminal paths exist.
Unused edges form a forest with degrees one and three, apart from isolated terminals.
A component with two leaves on either path produces a cycle avoiding the other path.
Hence every nonempty component is a matching edge between the two path interiors.
The forbidden 231, 312, and 321 patterns give disjoint terminal paths and cycles.
Their avoidance implies a concatenation of singletons and adjacent transpositions.
The argument remains valid with common endpoints at both ladder ends.

THREE-TERMINAL LEMMA
Suppressing the third terminal cannot create a loop in a saturated network.
Earlier bridge pieces cause a normalized loss of at least one half.
For the first piece, the prefix has rank p, giving A_p <= 2^p.
Its suffix transfer count is exactly 2^q.
The interface and singleton formulas give (1+A_p)/2^(p+1).
These ratios are one for p=0 and at most 3/4 otherwise.
For a square edge, the listed four cap-free paths and three cap-using paths
are complete and distinct.
The ratio is (4+3A_p)/2^(p+3).
It equals 7/8 for p=0 and is at most 5/8 for p>=1.
All square edges have the required symmetry.
No additional paths can leave and re-enter a suffix through its two-edge interface.
Such a path would use both interface edges and could not terminate on the right.

GENERAL INDUCTION
Off-route cyclic blocks lose a rank unit without adding terminal choices.
Multiple cyclic route blocks reduce by multiplication and induction.
In a two-connected block, deleting any vertex leaves a connected graph.
This gives uniform incident parity patterns with equal extension counts.
An internal degree at least four bounds the density by 7/8.
A terminal degree at least three bounds it by 3/4.
Suppressing unmarked degree-two vertices preserves rank and all terminal paths.
Adjacent terminals give at most 1+2^(b-1), which is at most (3/4)2^b for b>=2.
Otherwise, deleting one degree-two terminal leaves three distinct degree-two terminals.
Both branch counts have rank b-1.
If both are nonfull, induction applies to each.
If one is full, the three-terminal lemma bounds the other by 7/8 or makes it full.
This gives the sharp factor 15/16.
Loops may be treated as off-route cyclic blocks in the initial reduction.
