Status: the unrestricted concentration claim below is false.
Date: 2026-11-10.

Critical seven-cut profiles have all normalized pair-path counts tending to zero.
They have Boolean limits for four-terminal and six-terminal forest totals.
A narrower question may be easier than a uniform four-terminal forest gap:

If all six pair-path densities tend to zero and the four-terminal forest
total tends to one, must the forest pairing mass concentrate on one pairing?

A sufficient inequality would be
  f_i f_j <= C * 2^h * sum_ab P_ab
for distinct four-terminal pairings i,j, with an absolute constant C.
The attached-four-ladder construction below disproves this inequality.
Any replacement must use a structural condition such as the absence of small cyclic cuts.

Counterexample:
Start with a four-cycle whose vertices are the four ports.
Attach one saturated two-pole of rank b to each port by a bridge.
Retain the other terminal of each two-pole as the corresponding new port.
The resulting normalized four-pole has rank h=4b+1.
Its four-terminal forest total is exactly 2^h.
The two alternating four-cycle pairings each have mass 2^(h-1).
Every terminal-pair path count is exactly 2^(2b+1).
Thus all six normalized path counts equal 2^(-2b), but the pairing mass does not concentrate.
Each attached pole gives a cyclic two-edge cut in any cubic completion.
Its boundary is its attachment bridge together with its filled port edge.
The example does not address the restricted critical seven-cut problem.

The full-forest bridge family supports this possible conclusion.
Take two saturated capped singleton ladders of rank 2m+1.
Subdivide a central rung in each and join the new vertices by a bridge.
The four remaining ports are the original ladder endpoints.
The total rank is 4m+2.
Every four-terminal join omits the bridge and is a forest.
Its pairing always joins the two ports within each original ladder.
The within-ladder pair densities are 2^(-2m-1).
The cross-pair densities tend to zero by the central-rung path estimates.

The rank-eight unique-bad witness gives a different diagnostic.
Its four-forest count is 255/256 of the parity class.
Its six terminal-path counts are 219,143,168,168,143,128 out of 256.
Its girth is four.
Thus this near-full witness does not approach the critical zero-path regime.

One attempted proof uses switching between two forests of different pairings.
The needed output could be one simple terminal path and one unrestricted parity state.
This would give the correct counting scale P*2^h.
However, local switching can create a terminal-free cycle in the proposed path color.
Shared terminal edges also prevent changing source sets while preserving the edge multiset.
These are unresolved obstacles, not evidence against the inequality.

The fixed-girth theorem gives a useful additional restriction.
Any unresolved above-half family without cyclic cuts of size at most four
must have girth tending to infinity.
All current near-full four-forest switch witnesses have girth three or four.
