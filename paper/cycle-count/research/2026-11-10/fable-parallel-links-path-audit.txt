1. Parallel complementary components

Retaining one component joining A and B gives a connected core.
Deleting any two other complementary components leaves a connected graph.
The one-link pair law therefore makes their zero-boundary events independent.
Conditional internal nonzero-even events preserve pairwise independence.
Their probabilities are at least2^(-t-1), so the elementary second moment
gives at most2^(t+1) eligible regions when forest probability exceeds1/2.
The statement is correctly limited to these parallel components.

2. Refined moment

For total cycle weight mu and ordered two-link weight W2,

  E Z^2 <=mu^2+W2+(1+eta)mu.

One-link pairs are covered by the baseline mu^2.
Two-link pairs add W2, and the exceptional-partner bound handles >=3 links.
When mu>0 and forest probability exceeds1/2, this gives

  W2 >mu^2-(1+eta)mu.

At mu=0, only the nonstrict version is valid.
This vacuous edge case was corrected in our extension fragment.
All substantive applications have mu>=20.

3. Clean block geometry

The full block U_b minus(U_a union C_a) has boundary in the two separator cycles.
The separator cycles avoid both marked vertices and S0.
After discarding blocks that meet S0 or P, no retained block can have a neighbor there.
Discarding blocks meeting a neighbor of the anchor cycle removes the only
additional boundary edges absent from Gamma minus the anchor cycle.
Hence only S0 itself must be discarded, not its neighbors.

4. One-block spectral supply

For a block with n>=8L^2 and deficit d<=2L,

  Ld/(2n)<=1/8.

For even lengths6x..L, the normalized Perron contribution is at least7/8.
The block order is at most3r, so the nonreal error is at most9r^(-2).
The repeated-walk factor is1+o(1), uniformly over the blocks.

This argument does not require connected blocks or eigenvalue multiplicities.
