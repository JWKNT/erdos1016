1. Parallel topology and probability

Keep one component c0 joining the connected sets A and B. Then
K=A union B union c0 is connected. Every other component of Gamma-K
has an edge to K. Deleting any two such components leaves a connected
graph. Therefore their zero-boundary events have exactly one link in
the pair law, and are independent.

Given zero boundary, a component's internal cycle space is uniform.
For two closed components these internal choices are independent too.
Thus the events "closed boundary and nonzero internal even set" are
pairwise independent. A cyclic component with boundary at most t has
event probability at least2^(-t-1). The conditional nonzero probability
is1-2^(-beta), at least one half.

If m components remain after c0 is omitted, their event count N has
mean mu>=m2^(-t-1) and second moment at most mu^2+mu. Hence the forest
probability is at most1/(1+mu). A value above one half forces
m<2^(t+1). Reinstating c0 gives at most2^(t+1) qualifying components.
This bound needs no positive lower bound on the forest excess.

The stronger mutual-independence statement is also correct. Every cut
supported on the union of the selected component stars has all of K
on one side and decomposes into cuts supported on individual stars.
The pairwise statement alone suffices for the argument.

2. Small-cycle deletion input

Each chosen short cycle lies outside P and has cubic vertices, so its
vertex region has boundary at most its length. The exponential cut bound
  p <= 1/2 + 2 exp[-k/(8D2^D)]
and p>1/2+epsilon give
  k < 8D2^D ln(2/epsilon).
Multiplication by D gives |S|=O(x^3 2^D) for polynomially small epsilon.
The older inverse-k cut estimate instead gives
|S|=O(x^2 2^D/epsilon). Both uses are valid for variable D.

3. Second moment and the exact two-link family

For disjoint cycles, a one-link pair has normalized factor1 and a
two-link pair has factor2. The weighted contribution of at least
three links is at most eta*mu, by the exceptional-partner estimate.
Consequently
  E Z^2 <= mu^2 + W2 + (1+eta)mu,
where W2 is the ordered weight of exactly-two-link disjoint pairs.
The forest event forces Z=0. Its probability above one half gives
W2>mu^2-(1+eta)mu. The upper bound W2<=mu^2 also gives
mu<=(1+eta)/(4epsilon). Removing direct-edge pairs leaves two exterior
link components, as required for the separator-chain construction.

4. Restored clean blocks

Use the full slab V(a,b) in H=Gamma-C. Every H-edge leaving the slab
ends on one of its two separator cycles. These cycles avoid P and S0.
Thus a slab disjoint from P and S0 cannot have a neighbor in either set.
Discarding only slabs that meet S0 is sufficient. The earlier discard
of S0-neighbors was safe but unnecessary. Discarding slabs that meet
N(C) handles edges that were removed when H was formed.

Each retained block induces exactly the same graph in Gamma and J0.
Its vertices are cubic in Gamma. As a graph by itself, its degree
deficit equals its boundary, at most2L. Its girth exceeds D0.
Different blocks are vertex-disjoint, so their cycle collections are
disjoint even without a nonadjacency condition.

5. One-block spectral supply

Choose q=ceil(8L^2/(D1+1))+2. Then q=O(x), and each block has at least
8L^2 vertices. Its deficit d<=2L gives Ld/(2n)<=1/8.
Also n<=|Gamma|<=3r eventually. Use the manuscript's even-power window
[6x,L], rather than an unspecified window "about2x". The nonreal error
is then at most9r^(-2), uniformly over blocks.

The normalized Perron contribution is at least7/8-o(1). Repeated
nonbacktracking walks contribute a relative O(L^2 2^(-s0))=o(1).
Thus each block has cycle weight at least
  (7/32-o(1)) ln(A/6).
For A=6 exp(168), a uniform lower bound w0=1 is safe. This proves the
required linear supply in the number of blocks without multiplicity
counting or the matrix-pencil multi-mode lemma.

6. Fixed exponent arithmetic

For0<kappa<1/3, delta=(1-3kappa)/8 gives
 d1+kappa=1-2delta,
 d1/2-(d0+kappa)=delta,
 d1/2-kappa=3delta.
For0<kappa<1/2, delta=(1-2kappa)/8 gives
 d1=1-2delta,
 d1/2-d0=1/2-3delta>0,
 d1/2-kappa=3delta.
The new link count is at most(delta/2)x+O(1), whereas s0>=delta*x-2.
The exceptional-pair error therefore tends to zero at both scales.
The stated packing, restoration, and final moment contradictions pass.
State the useful ranges with kappa positive explicitly.

7. Root's endpoint proposal: PASS

Put x=log2 r, L=floor(Ax),
 t=floor(log2 x-2log2 log2 x),
 D0=ceil(8Ax/log2 x), D1=floor(x-5log2 x),
 epsilon=x^7/sqrt(r), with connected P and |P|<=r/x^2.
All quantities below concern sufficiently large r; A is fixed.

Then2^(t+1)=O(x/(log x)^2), and the tree-link term is
O((log x)^2). Since t~log2 x, the full link count is less than
3Ax/log2 x, while s0=4Ax/log2 x+O(1). Thus eta0=o(1), and eta1=o(1).
The tree-region condition2(t+2floor(Dj/8))<=Dj holds because t=o(D0).

The exponential deletion estimate gives
 |S1|=O(r/x^2), |S0|=r^o(1).
Hence L(|P|+|S1|)=o(r). Also
 2^s1=Omega(sqrt(r)/x^(5/2)),
 T=Omega(sqrt(r)/x^(11/2)),
 m=Omega(sqrt(r)/x^(13/2)),
where q=O(x). All discarded blocks are negligible.
The one-block supply contradicts
 mu0<=(1+o(1))sqrt(r)/(4x^7)
by a factor of order sqrt(x). The direct cubic recurrence therefore
gives an unrestricted O(2^r(log r)^7/sqrt(r)) cycle-count error.

Possible optimization, not claimed here as a completed theorem:
D1=x-4log2 x-B_A, with a sufficiently large fixed shift, keeps a fixed
small spectral loss and yields m=Omega_A(sqrt(r)/x^6). Choosing the
constant in epsilon=C_A x^6/sqrt(r) large enough may give logarithmic
power6. This requires tracking the fixed constants, not a new exponent
margin. The audited endpoint statement above uses the safer power7.

9. Strong-branch packing optimization, sent to root

The already audited exponential-cut proof has a stronger large-k branch:
if k>=2(5D+1)2^D, then
 p-1/2 <= (1/2)exp[-k/2^(D+1)].
Thus under p>1/2+epsilon,
 k < 2^(D+1) max(5D+1, ln(1/(2epsilon)))
up to replacing strictness by a weak bound at the threshold.
For D<=x and epsilon a fixed polylogarithm divided by sqrt(r), this is
O(x2^D), saving one logarithm over the weaker all-k estimate.
It gives |S1|=O(x^2 2^D1). Choosing D1=x-3log2 x-B suggests
O(2^r(log r)^(11/2)/sqrt(r)) by the same constant-loss argument.
This last optimization awaits its separate written proof and audit.
