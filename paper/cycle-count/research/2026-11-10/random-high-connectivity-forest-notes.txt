Date: 2026-11-10.
Status: a research route; no higher-terminal stability theorem is proved.

Target.
Fix an even terminal set T of bounded size in a normalized pole.
Investigate whether affine T-join forest density near one forces a cyclic cut
of bounded order in every cubic completion.
The remaining unrestricted cores have both girth and cyclic connectivity tending to infinity.

Exact translation observation.
Fix any T-join forest F0 and let Z be a uniform even subgraph.
Then J=F0 symmetric-difference Z is a uniform affine T-join.
Outside V(F0), the edge sets of J and Z agree.
Consequently
  Pr(J is a forest) <= Pr(Z induced outside V(F0) is a forest).
The marked-forest theorem therefore forces every T-join forest to be long
when the affine forest density is high.
This alone gives only the available marked-size scale, not additive stability.

Minimum-join structure.
Choose F0 with the fewest edges among all T-joins.
It is a forest, and in a normalized pole it is a union of |T|/2 disjoint paths.
For every cycle C,
  |C intersect F0| <= |C outside F0|.
Otherwise toggling C gives a shorter T-join.
Each path component is geodesic.
Two direct edges joining two distinct path components cannot have widely
separated endpoints: the two intervening F0 segments have total length at most two.
These restrictions remove many naive parallel-rail obstructions.

A possible dual organization.
The minimum T-join LP has the T-odd-cut constraints x(delta U)>=1.
Its dual packs T-odd cuts with total load at most one on each edge.
Standard uncrossing gives an optimal laminar dual support.
For an unweighted minimum join F0, complementary slackness then gives:
  each positive dual cut crosses F0 exactly once;
  each edge of F0 has total dual load exactly one.
After choosing one excluded root vertex, the laminar tree has at most |T| leaves,
since every inclusion-minimal positive cut contains a terminal.
Thus it has only O(|T|) branching nodes.
The induced weighted tree distance is at most one across every graph edge.
The F0 paths traverse its positive-weight edges without repetition.

This may replace ordinary distance layers by chains in a laminar cut tree.
Long unbranched chains have only one marked F0 crossing per cut.
A possible proof would first bound the branch regions, then apply the
one-path layer argument along the remaining chains.
The missing step is localization while preserving connected complement
when the marked forest has several components.
No bounded-width or additive-rank conclusion is currently justified.

Why a naive multi-path layer argument fails.
With q marked paths, the layer-window boundary can gain q times the span.
A cycle supplies only twice the span in vertices.
The minimum-span calculation that works for q=1 therefore does not directly
bound cycle length when q>=2.
The minimum-join restrictions or the laminar dual need to be used essentially.

Structural warning.
The four attached saturated ladders around C4 give a full four-terminal
forest space with two equal pairing masses and vanishing path densities.
Every attached pole has a cyclic two-edge boundary after completion.
Thus any general concentration theorem must use connectivity restrictions.
