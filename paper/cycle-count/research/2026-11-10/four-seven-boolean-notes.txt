Critical seven-shore state analysis.
10 November 2026.

Scope: these are limiting-state necessary conditions, not graph realizations.
The numerical solver supplies exploration, not an exact infeasibility proof.

The critical profile has zero path coordinates on both shores.
Each of 35 four-terminal sectors and 7 six-terminal sectors has common total
zero or one. Their 42 totals sum to 32.
The two shores have disjoint pairing supports in every four-sector.
Positive six-pairing supports across shores must have no common pair.
Each shore satisfies all current matching, star, binary-tree and
star-plus-edge cap constraints.

The script four-seven-boolean-feasibility.py encodes these conditions.
It imports the audited generic cap rows from random_seven_cycle_dual.py.
The original unsplit model had 777 variables, 357 binary, and 3848 rows.
Its 300-second run found neither a feasible point nor an infeasibility result.
The complete solver log is four-seven-boolean-feasibility.log.

A new exact cut helps the split models.
If a six-sector is active, at least three contained four-sectors are inactive.
To see this, choose positive matchings beta and gamma on opposite shores.
They are disjoint, so their union is a six-cycle.
Their common compatible matchings are the four perfect matchings of the
triangular prism complementary to that cycle in K6.
For any common compatible alpha, all three corresponding four-sectors
cannot be active: the three augmented path inequalities would require
two positive pairing slots on each shore among three disjoint slots.
Thus the inactive sectors, viewed as complementary pairs in this six-set,
must meet every one of the prism's four perfect matchings.
Each triangular-prism matching uses either all three spokes or one spoke
and one edge from each triangle. Two edges cannot hit all four matchings.
Therefore at least three inactive sectors are necessary.
The valid linear cut is sum(z_four inside S)+3*z_six(S)<=15.

The exact checker four-seven-prism-cuts.py tests all 120 compatible ordered
matching pairs and every possible two-edge blocker. It also lists all
minimum three-edge blockers by degree sequence.

Split models fix the number m of active six-sectors.
Port permutation makes any chosen m six-subsets equivalent.
The cases m=0,1,2 were numerically infeasible after 24.66, 25.56, and 16.76 seconds.
The m=3 run stopped after 180 seconds without a conclusion.
The m=7 run stopped after 300 seconds without a conclusion.
No feasible critical profile was found in these runs.
All searches were bounded and are now stopped.
