Second-order constructions: checkpoint 1, 2026-11-10

Status: symbolic proof below; independent finite checker passed.
The path-stability agent independently audited the proof and found no gap.

The proposed additive path bound with a constant C is false.
The counterexample uses the known Horak--Kim graph MC_(2q).
Delete one edge inside one of its q square blocks.
The resulting network N has rank b=2q and terminal distance ell=3.
Its exact path count is

  P_uv(N) = 2^(2q-1) + (q-3/4) 2^q + 1.

Thus (P_uv-2^(b-1))/2^((b+ell)/2) grows linearly in q.
This does not refute the proposed cycle error O(r 2^(r/2)).
The parent graph itself is the known lower-bound construction at that scale.

Primary source: Horak and Kim, arXiv:2006.13459v2, Section 5.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.13459
Theorem 5.1 gives the total cycle count. The marked-edge formula above
is a separate elementary count, not a quotation of that theorem.

Definition and proof.

For i modulo q, use four vertices a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i.
Put all four edges between {a_i,b_i} and {c_i,d_i} in block i.
Add c_i a_(i+1) and d_i b_(i+1) between consecutive blocks.
The graph is simple and cubic for q>=2. Its rank is 2q+1.
Fix the square edge e=a_0 c_0.

Every cycle uses an odd number of edges at every two-edge interface,
or an even number at every interface. This follows from cut parity.

Odd case: choose one interface edge independently at every interface.
Inside each square there are two paths between the selected endpoints.
This gives 4^q cycles. Exactly half contain e.
Indeed, among the four endpoint pairs and two local paths, four use e.
The contribution to c_e is therefore 2^(2q-1).

Even case, no used interface: only the square containing e contributes.
Its contribution is 1.

Even case, d used consecutive interfaces, 1<=d<=q-1:
The cycle occupies d+1 blocks. Each endpoint block has two possible caps.
Each internal block has two possible matchings. Thus there are 2^(d+1)
cycles for each interval. There are d+1 intervals containing block 0.
In each such interval, exactly half the cycles contain e.
The contribution is sum_(d=1)^(q-1) (d+1) 2^d = (q-1)2^q.

Even case, all q interfaces used:
Each block supplies one of its two matchings. A single cycle results
exactly when their product is the transposition. There are 2^(q-1)
such cycles. Half use e, so their contribution is 2^(q-2).

Adding gives the claimed formula. Deleting e converts these cycles
bijectively into simple paths between its endpoints. The other three
edges of its square form a terminal path. Bipartiteness gives distance 3.

Additional formula: if e is an interface edge, then
  c_e = 2^(2q-1) + (2q-7/2)2^q + 4.
For a proper interval of d interfaces, there are d positions containing e;
each contributes 2^(d+1). The all-interface case contributes 2^(q-1).
The interface-edge terminal distance need not stay bounded as q grows.

Consequences for future targets.

1. A constant-C bound with exactly exponent (b+ell)/2 cannot hold.
2. A polynomial factor, at least linear in b for ell=3, is necessary.
3. The weaker exponential-gap conjecture remains consistent with this family.
4. The cycle error O(r2^(r/2)) remains open; this example matches its scale.

Checkpoint 2: series extension and four-terminal block structure.

Append t two-terminal triangles in series to N_q. Each triangle has two
terminal paths and terminal distance one. The new simple graph has
  b=2q+t, ell=3+t,
  excess = 2^t ((q-3/4)2^q+1).
Thus a factor of order b-ell is necessary beside 2^((b+ell)/2).
This holds throughout the possible gap range, not only at distance three.

A new structural proposition is saved in second-order-four-poles.tex.
Let K have four degree-two ports L1,L2,R1,R2 and all other degrees three.
Write h=beta(K). Suppose that every cross count P_(Li,Rj) equals 2^h.
If two vertex-disjoint cross paths exist, then K is a permutation ladder.
Its matching permutation consists only of singleton blocks and adjacent swaps.
If there are a swaps, then its number of through pairs is exactly 2^a.
In particular it is at most 2^floor((h+1)/2).

The proof first shows that every cross path meets every cycle.
For two disjoint cross paths P,Q, all unused edges form a forest.
A forest component cannot meet either path twice. Otherwise its two
attachments produce a cycle disjoint from the other cross path.
The unused edges therefore form a perfect matching P-to-Q, with no unused
vertices. The graph is a permutation ladder. Patterns 231,312,321 each
give a two-rung cycle disjoint from a cross path. Avoiding these patterns
forces the claimed blocks. Local through choices then give 2^a.

The independent checker exhausts all permutations of orders 2 through 7.
It verifies both the saturation criterion and the exact through-pair count.
There are 2,3,5,8,13,21 saturated permutations, respectively.
This is a restricted construction barrier, not a general second-order theorem.
It does not classify saturated blocks whose through-pair count is zero.

Checkpoint 3: exact saturated-block bounds and a general saturation gap.

The exact three-pole maximum is now proved in second-order-saturated-blocks.tex.
For three degree-two terminals a,b,c, with all other vertices cubic and rank h,
assume P_ac=P_bc=2^h. Then
  P_ab <= M(h),
  M(2j)=3*2^j-2,  M(2j+1)=4*2^j-2.
Simple equality families exist for every h. The proof allows parallel edges.
The independent search and exact equality-family checks agree through h=12.

This gives c(N)<=10*2^(b/2) for saturated cubic two-terminal networks.
It also bounds caps and through pairs in every saturated four-terminal block.
For any cyclic chain of saturated four-terminal blocks, the cycle count lies
between 2^(r-1) and 2^(r-1)+20*r*2^(r/2).
The initial ring proof omitted a state with one double-cap block.
The independent audit found it. The corrected proof includes that state and
bounds its contribution by 3*(r-1)*2^((r-1)/2).
Both the exact three-pole theorem and corrected ring proof passed the
four-regular agent's independent audit.

The principal new result is in second-order-path-gap.tex:
  Every connected graph or multigraph of rank b has either exactly 2^b
  simple terminal paths, or at most (15/16)*2^b.
The constant is sharp for a simple rank-four graph with fifteen paths.

Proof structure:
1. A saturated bridgeless cubic two-pole consists of two terminal paths,
   with a perfect matching between their interiors. The matching permutation
   has only singleton and adjacent-swap blocks. Bridges join these pieces.
2. Subdivide any edge of such a full network to make a third terminal b.
   If the original terminal pair a,c remains full, the count from b to c
   is either full or at most 7/8 of full. Exact local formulas prove this.
3. Split a general cubic two-pole at one terminal. Each branch has rank b-1.
   If both branches are nonfull, use induction. If one is full, use step 2.
4. For general graphs, use the block route. In a two-connected block,
   a terminal of degree at least three gives density at most 3/4.
   An internal vertex of degree at least four gives density at most 7/8.
   Uniform incident parity patterns prove both statements.

The full general gap proof passed both the four-regular and path-stability
agents' line-by-line audits. The root's line-by-line audit also passed.
The independent checker passed 6330 subdivision formulas on all 231 capped
ladders with at most ten positions. It also checked the simple sharp witness.
The files are check_second_order_path_gap.py and second-order-path-gap-checks.json.
The path-stability agent also ran an independent checker with 874 matching
permutations and 522 saturated edge subdivisions; every assertion passed.

Consequence for the construction search: if all four cross-path densities
of a block tend to one, they eventually exceed 15/16 and are exactly full.
Thus vanishing defects cannot evade the saturated-ring half-exponent barrier.
No construction exceeding the general cycle scale r*2^(r/2) was found.

Checkpoint 4: arbitrary rings and four-terminal forest searches.

The new fragment second-order-general-rings.tex treats arbitrary four-pole
rings with at least eleven blocks. If any block is not cross-saturated, the
total cycle count is strictly below 2^(r-1). Otherwise the audited saturated
ring bound applies. The proof combines the path gap with a transfer trace.
One nonfull entry loses at least 1/64 of the odd-interface sector.
The entire even sector has relative size at most
  (2k+1)*2^(-k)+k*2^(-2k+1),
which is below 1/64 for k>=11. The key all-used-interface estimate is
D_i+C_i<=2^h_i, where D_i counts through pairs and C_i double caps.
The independent audit found a missing s=k interval, which omits one
interface but visits every block. The proof now includes that case.
It changes the safe block threshold from ten to eleven.
The corrected full proof passed the four-regular agent's independent audit.

Checkpoint 5: one-full tripole sum.

The new fragment second-order-one-full-tripole.tex proves:
  P_ac=2^h implies P_ab+P_bc<=2^h+6*2^(h/2)
for connected cubic three-poles, allowing parallel edges.
Suppress b and use the full two-pole classification.
Prefix cap counts obey A_p<=3*2^(p/2)-2.
For interface and singleton edges, the normalized terms are
f_p=(1+A_p)/2^(p+1), and f_p<=1/2 for p>=3.
For square edges, use g_p=(4+3A_p)/2^(p+3), with g_p<=1/2 for p>=2.
When both ends are long, the sum is at most full.
Otherwise the short prefix gives a half-exponent bound for the opposite count.
Series bridge chains preserve the bound through
  A+lambda*B <= lambda*2^t+6*2^(t/2).
If both sides have positive rank, each count is at most half of full.
The proof passed both the four-regular and path-stability independent audits.
The root is using it in the universal tripole sum and three-edge-cut theorem.
Independent DFS checks passed 6330 capped-ladder subdivisions through ten
positions, plus all one-sided bridge weights with side ranks one through eight.
The reusable checker is check_second_order_one_full_tripole.py.

Checkpoint 6: four-edge cuts.

The exact gluing identity is in second-order-four-cut-states.tex.
For four-pole shores of ranks h,k, the total rank is h+k+3.
The crossing cycles are the dot product of six path states, plus
F_X*F_Y minus the dot product of the three forest-pairing states.
Equal forest pairings give two cycles, so their contribution must be removed.

The same fragment extends the thin-layer encoding to unions of at most
two vertex-disjoint cycles. It gives O_epsilon(b^4*2^(b/2)) such unions
in a two-pole whose path density exceeds one half by epsilon.
Capping both sides of a four-pole maps all its terminal forests injectively
to these unions. Thus a cross-path sum above(2+epsilon)*2^h bounds the
forest count by O_epsilon(h^4*2^(h/2)).
The path-stability agent independently audited this complete argument.

second-order-four-cut-large-shores.tex proves the half-exponent estimate
O(r^8*2^(r/2)) when both shore ranks exceed an absolute threshold.
Both the four-regular and path-stability agents independently audited it.
Its finite profile classification has three exceptional supports:
three full pairs forming a star; four full pairs forming K2,2; and two
disjoint full pairs. An added-edge argument concentrates the last type's
forest mass on the corresponding pairing. All nonaligned cases have a fixed
deficit. Aligned stars and K2,2 have half-exponent errors.

second-order-one-full-tripole.tex now also proves
  P_ac=2^h implies min(P_ab,P_bc)<=3*2^(3h/4).
The four-regular agent independently verified this uniform minimum bound.

second-order-all-four-cuts.tex completes the bounded-shore argument.
It proves the universal aggregate
  sum of six path counts + total four-terminal forests
  <=4*2^h+O(h^4*2^(h/2)).
The proof uses the added-edge coupling and the new minimum bound.
The C4 shore requires its exact forest coefficients(1/2,1/2,1), which
give the needed crossing deficit when the large shore has low path counts.
The resulting theorem covers every CYCLIC cut of size at most four.
Cyclicity cannot be dropped: every cubic graph has an acyclic two-vertex
shore with four boundary edges.
The final fragment passed the root, four-regular, and path-stability audits.
The added-edge lemma's rank-threshold assumption is now explicit.
The exact finite checker verifies the gluing identity, the added-edge path
identity, and the capping map on individual forest edge sets.

Checkpoint 7: the sharp order for cyclic cuts of size at most four.

The four-regular agent refined the two-cycle packing factor from b^4 to b.
Its proof sums bounded-overlap layer intervals with their geometric weights.
The same-side cap product cancels for aligned saturated K2,2 shores.
Together these prove
  c(G)<=2^(r-1)+K*r*2^(r/2)
for every connected loopless cubic graph with a cyclic cut of size at most four.
