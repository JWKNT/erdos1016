\section{A prism obstruction for active six-terminal sectors}
\label{sec:four-seven-prism-obstruction}

This section concerns a critical pair of limiting seven-pole profiles.
All path coordinates vanish, and corresponding forest totals are the same Boolean values on both shores.
Corresponding four-terminal pairing supports are disjoint.
Positive six-terminal pairings on opposite shores have no common pair.
These are the equality conditions for the normalized crossing bound $32$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:four-seven-active-six-three-missing}
In a critical pair of limiting profiles, each active six-terminal sector contains at least three inactive four-terminal sectors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix an active six-set $S$.
Choose positive six-terminal pairing masses $w_X(\beta)$ and $w_Y(\gamma)$ on the two shores.
Their matchings $\beta,\gamma$ have no common pair.
Their union is therefore a six-cycle.

For a perfect matching $\alpha=\{e,f,g\}$ of $S$, write
\[
 R_X(\alpha)=\sum_{\eta\cap\alpha=\varnothing}w_X(\eta),
 \qquad
 R_Y(\alpha)=\sum_{\eta\cap\alpha=\varnothing}w_Y(\eta).
\]
Suppose $\alpha$ has no pair in common with either $\beta$ or $\gamma$.
Then both displayed quantities are positive.
The three four-sectors $e\cup f,e\cup g,f\cup g$ cannot all be active.
Indeed, the three matching-augmentation inequalities on either shore would give
\[
 u_{e|f}+u_{e|g}\ge R(\alpha),\qquad
 u_{e|f}+u_{f|g}\ge R(\alpha),\qquad
 u_{e|g}+u_{f|g}\ge R(\alpha).
\]
Thus at least two of these three pairing coordinates would be positive on each shore.
This contradicts disjoint corresponding supports.

The graph $K_6-(\beta\cup\gamma)$ is a triangular prism.
Its four perfect matchings are the common compatible matchings $\alpha$ above.
An inactive four-subset $T\subset S$ corresponds to the complementary pair $S\setminus T$.
The preceding paragraph says that these complementary pairs meet every perfect matching of the prism.

Label the prism's two triangles compatibly, with three joining spokes.
Its perfect matchings are the three spokes together and three further matchings.
Each further matching contains one spoke and the opposite edge of each triangle.
A triangle edge belongs to one perfect matching, while a spoke belongs to two.
Any two spokes belong together to the all-spoke matching.
Consequently two edges meet at most three of the four perfect matchings.
At least three complementary pairs, and therefore three inactive four-sectors, are required.
\end{proof}

For Boolean sector flags, the lemma gives the valid inequalities
\[
 \sum_{\substack{T\subset S\\|T|=4}}z_T+3z_S\le15
 \qquad(|S|=6).
\]
The standard-library checker \texttt{four-seven-prism-cuts.py} verifies all $120$ compatible ordered pairs $\beta,\gamma$.
It tests every possible two-edge blocker and records all minimum three-edge blockers.
The proof does not depend on that finite verification.
