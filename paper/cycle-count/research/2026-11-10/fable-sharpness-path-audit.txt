This is a mathematical audit, not Lean verification.

1. Pair enumeration and tilted law.
The local states in Lemma 2 cover all even possibilities, including loops.
When a spoke is used, exactly one of its two free points is used. At a
cubic vertex, a used loop is represented by its two used points and its
unique unused point. A T-join on W gives exactly the required spoke parity.
The used and unused matchings therefore give the claimed bijection.

The exact Radon--Nikodym relation is

  E_nu f = E_pi[2^(comp(G)-1) E_xi f] / (1+eta).

Since the weight is at least one, both inequalities in Lemma 4 have the
stated direction. No connectivity assumption is silently imposed here.

2. Component moments.
A closed set of cubic vertices is exactly a union of components not
meeting W. The parity restriction y even is necessary and included.
The denominator bound follows by selecting the indicated vertex groups
in a binomial coefficient with the correct total size. For the first
moment, j<=N-3 because m>=3. Thus all j>=2 terms are bounded by
2/[N(N-1)], while j=1 contributes 1/N. The stated 3/N bound follows.
The reciprocal-binomial sum for n0/2>=4 is at most three, proving the
second component-moment bound.

3. Spoke distribution.
The image of the spoke map consists exactly of subsets even in every
S-containing component of J. It is a product of uniform even-subset laws.
Using the first a-1 bits in each component gives a binomial variable with
m-q trials dominated by tau. For q<=m/2, the threshold m/8 is at most
one quarter of the number of trials. Hoeffding therefore gives exp(-m/16).
The tilted bad-component term is correctly bounded using
comp(J)=comp(G)-1+q and the second component moment.

4. Long and short cycles.
Lemma 3 counts cycles correctly at lengths one and two, as well as longer
lengths. When tau>=m/8, the denominator 2k+tau-1 is below 2(n0+m), which
gives the claimed damping parameter. Summing (1-kappa)^t/(2t) first, then
using (1-kappa)^g<=exp(-kappa g), gives the exact factor 1/(2g kappa).
The alternative sqrt(k)/t bound covers the rare small-tau event.

The short-cycle first moment uses the ordered point-pair counts 6n0+2m.
The hypothesis 4g^2<=M controls the pairing denominator by a factor below e.
Summing t times the cycle expectation gives the stated 2^(g+1) bound.

5. Cleanup and monotonicity.
Removing the spokes at a cycle can create at most one component outside
the W-component, because all removed outside endpoints remain connected
by that cycle. This proves Lemma 9.

Lemma 10 only subdivides J, so it creates no new cycles of J. The four
rank comparisons after deleting an image cycle are correct. In the case
with one endpoint on the cycle, the new two-edge path can merge components,
which only decreases its rank contribution. In the chord case, the new
vertex is a leaf. Both cases satisfy the required upper bound.

Every original loop and parallel pair is short. Subdividing each of its
edges at least twice removes loops and parallel edges in the final graph.
All added spoke endpoints on W are distinct. The result is simple, cubic,
and connected. Further attachment operations preserve these properties.

6. Choice of a good pairing and constants.
The two Markov failures each have probability at most 1/4. Connectivity
fails with probability at most 6/n0. Their sum is below one eventually.
The cleanup contribution is at most delta/4. The long-cycle contribution
is at most (1+eta)delta/4+o(1), so there is ample room below delta.
All comparisons involving C0=41 max(2,ln(4/delta)) are valid.

7. Exact rank and marked size.
The cleanup cost K*2^(g+3)=O(sqrt(n0) log n0) is o(m). The target-rank
budget has jumps of this same order when ceil(log n0/2) increases.
Its other increments are bounded. Thus choosing the largest admissible
even n0 leaves an o(m) gap, including the difference between the budgeted
and actual cleanup costs. Each additional attachment increases rank and
marked-cycle length by exactly one. This supplies every sufficiently
large integer rank while preserving the asserted size constant.

Wording caveat, not a theorem gap.
Remark 8 says the proof gives p(G,W)->1. For a fixed delta and its fixed
coefficient C_delta, the argument proves p>=1-delta. Convergence to one
can be obtained by choosing delta tending to zero sufficiently slowly;
then C_delta also grows. Such a diagonal sequence can still have |W|=o(r).
The proof does not establish p->1 for one fixed coefficient in r/log r.
