MAIN NEW STRUCTURAL REDUCTION
For an absolute K_* and every K>=K_*, a sufficiently large connected loopless
cubic minimal-rank counterexample to
  c(G) <= 2^(r-1) + K*r*2^(r/2)
must have girth g and minimum cyclic cut order k satisfying
  .5 log_2 log_2 r -5.5 log_2 log_2 log_2 r -C3 < k=g
      <2 log_2 log_2 r +2 log_2 log_2 log_2 r +C4.
Every minimum cyclic cut must isolate a shortest cycle.
The minimal-counterexample statement explicitly assumes the proposed bound
for ALL connected graphs of smaller rank. It is a reduction, not a proof
that such a counterexample cannot exist.
Primary packet: paper/cycle-count/research/2026-11-10/all-fixed-cut-cycle-bound.tex/.pdf.
The title inside the packet is "Structural reductions for the second-order cycle bound".

SHARP ERROR FOR EVERY FIXED CYCLIC CUT ORDER
For every fixed K, connected loopless cubic multigraphs with a cyclic cut of
size at most K satisfy
  c(G) <= 2^(r-1) + O_K(r*2^(r/2)).
For fixed minimum cyclic cut order k>=5, the stronger estimate is
  c(G) <= 2^(r-1) - 2^(r-k-1) + o_k(2^r).
The K-dependence cannot be dropped from the proved statement.
Proof: path-fixed-cut-induction.tex. Delete one edge of a minimum cut and
suppress its endpoints. The minimum cyclic order drops by exactly one;
the exact cycle identity and uniform terminal-path estimate halve the known
five-cut deficit at each step.
The separate six-cut proof is preserved in six-cut-sharp-bound.tex/.pdf.
It covers both large shores and every bounded-shore case independently.

QUANTITATIVE LOCALIZATION AND PATH STABILITY
The retained region in Fable's localization construction need not be connected;
only its complement must be connected. Its localized rank is
  beta(R)+boundary(R)-components(R)+1 = (|R|+boundary(R))/2+1
when R is cubic. Closing around the connected marker now excludes every good
set directly, avoiding the old exponential component-packing step.
Consequently the additive terminal-path constant can be chosen with
  C_epsilon <= exp(O(epsilon^-2 * log^11(1/epsilon))).
The normalized edge-deletion path excess in cubic completions with no cyclic
cut <=4 is
  O((log log r)^(11/2)/sqrt(log r)).
This proves a strict below-half bound through minimum cut orders
  k <= .5 log log r -5.5 log log log r -C.
Proof: path-quantitative-localization.tex.

UNIFORM HIGHER-TERMINAL FOREST STABILITY
For a connected normalized k-pole of rank h>=2 and girth g>=k, every even
terminal subset T has affine forest density at most
  1/2 + C*g^(13/2)*2^(-g/4).
The estimate is uniform in h,k,T. A cycle pole of rank one is an essential
exception. The proof uses a connected BFS-tree marker with at most k vertices
per distance layer, disconnected localization, a generalized growing-window
bound, and a port-free cubic ball supplied by an elementary entropy-Moore bound.
Proofs: path-high-girth-terminal-forests.tex and
four-high-connectivity-entropy-moore.tex.
If a minimum cyclic cut has two noncycle shores, this yields
  c(G)/2^r <= 1/4 + O(g^(13/2)*2^(-g/4)).
Hence every above-half sequence without cyclic <=4 cuts eventually has k=g,
and all its minimum cuts isolate shortest cycles.
A weighted-kernel extension shows that, for every fixed C, an above-half family
with growing girth cannot have a cut <=C*g with two connected shores of ranks
at least H_C=ceil(64(C+1)log_2(16(C+1)))+1.
Proof: four-high-connectivity-weighted-kernel.tex.

GIRTH WINDOWS AND THE NO-DELETION CHAIN
A Moore-ball argument bounds a normalized k-pole geodesic by
  g*(k+1+n*2^(-g/2)).
The endpoint forest theorem remains valid for a connected marker of size
  a*r/log_2 r, with a=1/(1000*6*exp(168)).
These facts make the shortest-cycle prefix argument quantitative. Without
any cyclic-connectivity assumption, sufficiently large cubic graphs satisfy
  c(G) <= half -2^(r-g-1) + O(2^r*(log r)^(11/2)/sqrt(r))
when g>=2 log log r+2 log log log r+C. Thus they lie strictly below half for
  g <= .5 log r -5.5 log log r -C'.
Proofs: four-high-connectivity-moore-paths.tex,
second-order-random-girth-window.tex, second-order-random-larger-marker.tex.
A separate selected-edge argument extends the cut induction up to logarithmic
cut orders when girth exceeds the cut order by (2+eta)log log r.
Proof: path-girth-surplus-cuts.tex.

At girth >=c log r, Fable's nested-chain argument works with no short-cycle
packing or deletion. A positive marked-forest excess, however small, supplies
a polynomial-size chain. A middle separator gives two large cyclic cores;
a minimum cut separating these cores has connected normalized shores and
preserves both rank lower bounds. Uniform affine forest bounds then force
cycle density at most1/4+o(1).
Therefore, if c(G)>=half, every edge of a shortest cycle has at most2^(r-2)
cycles. The predecessor-rank induction excludes logarithmic-girth minimal
counterexamples. Together with the girth window, this proves the main
structural reduction above.
Proof: second-order-random-no-deletion-chain.tex.

FAILED SHORTCUTS AND EXPLORATORY NOTES
A minimum cyclic cut need not contain an edge on a short cycle. For every
fixed k>=5 there are arbitrarily large simple cubic graphs of girth>=k+1,
with a unique minimum cyclic k-cut whose edges only lie on cycles of length
Omega_k(r). An explicit k=5 honeycomb construction has rank40M+6 and minimum
cut-edge cycle length at least2M+2. The primary external inputs are cited
inside second-order-random-long-minimum-cut.tex and
four-high-connectivity-long-cut.tex.
The existing Horak-Kim path construction rules out a constant-prefactor
2^((b+distance)/2) path error; a linear rank factor is necessary.
The general polynomial-prefactor exponential rank-distance bound remains open.
Numerical terminal-state optimization, transfer experiments, and quotient
interlace models remain exploratory. They are not proofs of the sharp bound.
A further excess-free packing extraction is being investigated; do not treat
an unfinished extraction argument as an unrestricted theorem.
