The distant-port gluing lemma handles arbitrary disconnected restricted
shores. If both are forests, the pole rank is internal-boundary minus
their component counts plus one, contradicting rank at least k. Each
component of the unique forest shore contains at most one port, since
its boundary is order+2-number_of_ports and port distance is at least2k.
Equal cyclic-bulk sides give an acyclic opposite global side. Opposite
bulk sides give cut size at least k+alpha+beta. Equality then forces
both forest shores empty. This proves uniqueness in the strict internal
connectivity case.

The iterative chain construction preserves cyclic connectivity K=k+1.
Every new attachment is distant from all preceding attachment ports in
its terminal block, so passing through the old chain cannot create a
short path between new ports. Subdivision preserves internal cyclic
connectivity. The final rank is
L(n0+2K)-2K+k+1=L(n0+2K)-k-1,
as stated. The only external existence input is the stated Wormald
random-regular consequence; this audit does not independently verify
that external paper. The explicit honeycomb k=5 construction avoids it.

Quantitative caveat for future work:
The diagonal obstruction allows the cut order to grow arbitrarily
slowly. It does not contradict a prescribed fast-growing girth bound.
Moore-ball packing along a shortest terminal path in G-e gives
distance O(r*g*2^(-g/2)), where g is the girth. Thus sufficiently large
g relative to loglog r again guarantees sublinear edge-cycle lengths.
This observation is a proof sketch, not a separately audited theorem.
