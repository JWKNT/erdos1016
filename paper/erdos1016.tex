\begin{abstract}
For $n\ge3$, let $n+h(n)$ be the least number of edges in a graph on $n$ vertices that contains a cycle of every length from $3$ to $n$. Bondy stated in 1971 that $\log_2(n-1)-1\le h(n)\le\log_2n+\log_*n+O(1)$. Erd\H{o}s Problem \#1016 asks whether the lower bound can increase to match the upper bound. We prove that $h(n)=\log_2 n+\log_*n+O(1)$.

The main estimate concerns a connected graph with large cycle rank. Remove a union of cycles with few edges and few components. Restrict a uniformly random even edge set to the remaining edges. The probability that this restriction is a forest is at most $1/2+o(1)$. This estimate gives a near-halving recurrence for the normalized cycle-length capacity. Iteration gives the $\log_*n$ term.

A Lean~4 formalization verifies the proof.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{MSC 2020:} 05C38 (primary); 05C35, 05C50, 60C05 (secondary).\\
\textit{Keywords:} pancyclic graphs, cycle space, nonbacktracking matrix, second moment method, Erd\H{o}s problems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================
A graph on $n$ vertices is \emph{pancyclic} if it contains a cycle of every length $3,4,\ldots,n$. Let $n+h(n)$ be the least number of edges of a pancyclic simple graph on $n$ vertices, and let $\lstar x$ be the least $j\ge0$ such that $j$ applications of $\log_2$ take $x$ to a value at most one.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is an absolute constant $A$ such that, for every $n\ge3$,
\[
 \log_2n+\lstar n-A\le h(n)\le\floor{\log_2n}+\lstar n+1.
\]
In particular, $h(n)=\log_2n+\lstar n+O(1)$.
\end{theorem}

Bondy \cite{Bondy} stated without proof that $\log_2(n-1)-1\le h(n)\le\log_2n+\lstar n+O(1)$. A cycle count gives the lower bound. A connected graph with $n+h$ edges has at most $2^{h+1}-1$ cycles. A pancyclic graph needs $n-2$ of these cycles.

Griffin \cite{Griffin} gave a written proof and computed small values. Sridharan \cite{Sridharan} and George, Khodkar and Wallis \cite{GKW} give constructions. Alon and Krivelevich \cite{AK} give constructions for random graphs. Can the lower bound increase to $\log_2n+\lstar n-O(1)$? Does even $h(n)-\log_2n\to\infty$ hold? These are the questions in Erd\H{o}s Problem \#1016 \cite{ErdosProblems}.

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} answers both questions.

\subsection{The main estimate and an outline}\label{sec:outline}
A cycle count does not use the requirement that the lengths fill an interval. The following estimate supplies the additional constraint. For a graph $G$, let $\cZ(G)$ be its binary cycle space, the even edge sets of $G$, of dimension $\beta(G)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+\cc(G)$. Let $\xi$ be uniform on $\cZ(G)$, and for an edge set $M$ put
\[
 P_G(M)=\Prob(\xi\cap M\text{ is a forest}).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Forest estimate]\label{thm:forest}
There is $r_0$ such that the following holds for all $r\ge r_0$. Let $G$ be a connected simple graph with $\beta(G)=r$, and let $W\subseteq G$ be a nonempty union of cycles with $m=|E(W)|\le\sqrt r$ edges and $c=\cc(W)\le(\log_2r)/100$ components. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forest-main}
 P_G\bigl(E(G)\setminus E(W)\bigr)\le\frac12+\frac{20}{\log^{(3)}r}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Many parts of a graph can share one parity bit. That bit can make all these parts acyclic with probability $1/2$. This explains the threshold. Section~\ref{sec:cuts} gives the zero-cut correlation ratio for two regions: $2^{\ell-1}$. Here $\ell$ counts the links between the regions. The section also shows that many disjoint small cyclic regions reduce the forest probability to approximately $1/2$.

Section~\ref{sec:forest} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:forest}. We reduce the maximum degree to three and keep $W$ as a marked set with few components. We then delete short cycles. The nonbacktracking spectrum supplies many moderately long cycles. We apply the second moment method to the events that such a cycle is a component of $\xi$.

Equation \eqref{eq:links} gives the main link bound. At most $c+o(\log r)$ components link two short cycles. Thus the witness components control all correlations. The proof needs no expansion assumption.

Section~\ref{sec:recurrence} uses Theorem~\ref{thm:forest} to prove the recurrence
\[
 \Phi\bigl(2^{256R}\bigr)\le\Bigl(\frac12+\frac{20}{\log^{(2)}R}\Bigr)\Phi(R)+2^{2-R}
\]
for the normalized cycle-length capacity $\Phi$. Each step increases the rank by one exponential level and reduces $\Phi$ by approximately one half. The errors are summable. Thus $\Phi(r)=O(2^{-\lstar r})$, which gives the lower bound. Section~\ref{sec:upper} gives the construction.

\subsection{Conventions}\label{sec:notation}
All graphs are finite. Every feasible prescribed-boundary class of edge sets has $2^{\beta(G)}$ members. Regions are induced, $P_G(H)=P_G(E(H))$, and a region is \emph{cyclic} if it contains a cycle. For a region $A$, $\delta A$ is its cut, and ``$\delta A=0$'' is the event that $\xi$ avoids it. Auxiliary graphs may have loops and parallel edges, with loops counted twice in degrees. All logs are binary except $\ln$; $\log^{(j)}t$ means $j$ successive binary logarithms, and $\lstar$ stops at one.

\section{Cut correlations}\label{sec:cuts}
%======================================================================
For a nonnegative random variable $Z$ with $\mu=\E Z>0$ and a constant $K\ge0$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment}
 \E Z^2\le2\mu^2+K\mu\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Prob(Z=0)\le1-\frac{\mu^2}{\E Z^2}\le\frac12+\frac K{4\mu+2K}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exceptional cuts]\label{lem:exceptional}
In a connected graph, let $A$ be a connected region with cut size $d$. Among connected regions $B$ disjoint from $A$ whose zero-cut events satisfy
\[
 \Prob(\delta A=\delta B=0)>2\,\Prob(\delta A=0)\,\Prob(\delta B=0),
\]
no vertex-disjoint subfamily has more than $d$ members.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Contract $A$ and a disjoint subfamily of the $B$'s to vertices. The cycle-space projection is onto. Thus it preserves their joint cut law. For two vertices $v,w$, count as \emph{links} the $vw$ edges and the components of $G-\{v,w\}$ attached to both. If there are $\ell$ links, rank subtraction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 \Prob(\delta v=\delta w=0)=2^{\ell-1}\,\Prob(\delta v=0)\,\Prob(\delta w=0).
\end{equation}

Fix $v$. Append its $d$ incidences as terminal leaves to a spanning forest of $G-v$. Prune nonterminal leaves. Each link to $w$ gives a branch at $w$ that contains a terminal. Thus each vertex with at least three links is a branch vertex of the pruned forest. This forest has at most $d$ branch vertices.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Small cuts]\label{cor:small-cuts}
For $k\ge1$ vertex-disjoint connected cyclic regions $H_1,\ldots,H_k$ with cuts at most $d$ in a connected graph $G$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-cuts}
 P_G\Bigl(\,\bigcup_iE(H_i)\Bigr)\le\frac12+\frac{(d+1)2^d}{2k}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $E_i$ say that the $i$th cut is zero and its inner even set is nonempty. Its probability $p_i$ is at least $2^{-d-1}$. Given zero cuts, the inner even sets are independent. Thus the pairwise correlation ratio equals the zero-cut ratio. By Lemma~\ref{lem:exceptional}, each event has at most $d$ exceptional partners in this disjoint family.

For the other pairs, use $\Prob(E_iE_j)\le2p_ip_j$. For exceptional partners, use $\Prob(E_iE_j)\le p_i$. Thus $Z=\sum_i\ind_{E_i}$ satisfies
\[
 \mu\ge k2^{-d-1},\qquad\E Z^2\le2\mu^2+(d+1)\mu.
\]
Each $E_i$ excludes a forest. Thus \eqref{eq:second-moment} proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the forest estimate}\label{sec:forest}
%======================================================================
Let $G$ and $W$ satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:forest}, with $r$ large. Set
\[
 x=\log r,\qquad y=\log x,\qquad z=\log y,\qquad D=\floor{x/10},\qquad L=\floor{x\sqrt y},
\]
and suppose the probability in \eqref{eq:forest-main} exceeds $\frac12+\eps$, where $\eps=20/z$. We obtain a contradiction. All estimates below are uniform under the two witness bounds.

\subsection{Preserve witness components during degree reduction}\label{sec:cubic}
Replace each vertex of degree $d>3$ by a path of $d-2$ cubic vertices. Put two old incidences at each end. Put one old incidence at each internal vertex. If $k\ge2$ incidences belong to $W$, put these incidences first along the path. Mark the initial segment that meets them. This segment is connected and has at most $k$ vertices.

Also mark unchanged witness vertices. In the expanded graph $G'$, the witness lifts to a subgraph on a marked set $P$ with
\[
 |P|\le2m,\qquad\cc(G'[P])\le c.
\]

Contraction of the splitting trees gives a cycle-space bijection. Lift an original forest to the expanded graph. Add all tree edges to this lift. The result is still a forest. Thus restriction to the complement of $P$ can only increase the forest probability.

Prune unmarked leaves. Suppress unmarked degree-two vertices. Their even coordinates are zero at leaves and equal along suppressed paths, respectively. These operations preserve the cycle-space law and the forest event outside $P$. They remove no marked vertex. Suppression can join marked components, but cannot split them.

Call the resulting connected multigraph $\Gamma$. It has rank $r$ and maximum degree three. Every unmarked vertex is cubic. The following bounds hold:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic}
 |P|\le2\sqrt r,\qquad\cc(\Gamma[P])\le c,\qquad|\Gamma|=2r+O(\sqrt r),\qquad P_\Gamma(\Gamma-P)>\tfrac12+\eps.
\end{equation}
The order identity follows from $|\Gamma|=2r-2+\sum_v(3-\deg_\Gamma v)$. Only marked vertices contribute to this sum.

\subsection{Delete short cycles without connections between them}\label{sec:short}
Suppose $\Gamma-P$ contains $\ceil{\sqrt r}$ disjoint cycles of length at most $D$, including loops and digons. Their induced regions have cuts at most $D$. Then \eqref{eq:small-cuts} gives $P_\Gamma(\Gamma-P)\le\frac12+O(xr^{-2/5})$. This is a contradiction.

Otherwise, choose a maximal such packing. Let $S$ be the union of its vertex sets. Then $|S|\le D\sqrt r$, and
\[
 J=\Gamma-(P\cup S)
\]
is simple with girth greater than $D$, order $v=(2+o(1))r$, and deficit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deficit}
 d_J:=\sum_{u\in J}(3-\deg_Ju)\le3(|P|+|S|)=O(\sqrt r\log r).
\end{equation}
The proof does not require $P$ or $P\cup S$ to be connected.

\subsection{Supply cycle weight}\label{sec:supply}
Let $B$, $A_0$ and $\Delta$ be the directed-edge nonbacktracking, adjacency and degree matrices of $J$, respectively. Set $M(t)=t^2I-tA_0+\Delta-I$. Compare this with the Ihara--Bass identity \cite{Bass,KS}. Then
\[
 M(2)=2(\Delta-A_0)+3I-\Delta\succeq0,\qquad\mathbf 1^TM(2-d_J/v)\,\mathbf 1=0.
\]

The least eigenvalue of the real symmetric matrix $M(t)$ is continuous. It is nonpositive at $2-d_J/v$ and nonnegative at $2$. Thus $M(t)$ is singular for some $t\in[2-d_J/v,2]$, with $t>1$. A null vector $u$ gives the nonzero $B$-eigenvector $f(i,j)=tu_j-u_i$.

Conversely, a nonreal eigenvalue $t$ of $B$ gives $M(t)u=0$ with $u\ne0$. Multiplication by $u^*$ gives a real quadratic with constant-to-leading ratio at most two. Thus $|t|\le\sqrt2$. Even powers of real eigenvalues are nonnegative. Therefore
\[
 2^{-\ell}\tr B^\ell\ge\bigl(1-d_J/(2v)\bigr)^\ell-3v\,2^{-\ell/2}\qquad(\ell\text{ even}).
\]
By \eqref{eq:deficit}, the sum over even $6x\le\ell\le L$ gives
\[
 T:=\sum_{\ell\le L}\frac{\tr B^\ell}{2\ell\,2^\ell}\ge\bigl(\tfrac14+o(1)\bigr)\ln\frac L{6x}=\bigl(\tfrac18+o(1)\bigr)\ln y.
\]

Set $s=\floor{(D-1)/2}$. The girth bound makes an $s$-step nonbacktracking path between fixed endpoints unique. Let $N_t(u,w)$ count ordinary nonbacktracking walks of length $t$ from $u$ to $w$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:walks}
 2^{-t}N_t(u,w)\le\tfrac32\,2^{-s}\qquad(t>s).
\end{equation}

Let $V=\sum_{C\subset J,\,|C|\le L}2^{-|C|}$, over simple cycles. A nonsimple cyclically nonbacktracking walk contains a proper simple cycle. Its complementary ordinary closed nonbacktracking walk has length greater than $D$. Encode the walk by the cycle, an oriented start, the complement and the original root. The factor $1/(2\ell)$ cancels orientations and root positions. The start vertex and complementary length contribute at most $L^2$.

Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collisions}
 0\le T-V\le\tfrac32L^22^{-s}V,\qquad V\ge\bigl(\tfrac18+o(1)\bigr)\ln y.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Normalize cycle indicators}\label{sec:indicators}
Consider each cycle $C\subset J$ of length at most $L$. Let $I_C$ be the event that $C$ is a component of the random even set. Set
\[
 a_C=2^{-|C|},\qquad p_C=\Prob(I_C),\qquad X_C=(a_C/p_C)\ind_{I_C},\qquad Z=\sum_CX_C.
\]
Cubic ambient degree makes $I_C$ exactly the event that the even set contains all cycle edges. These binary linear conditions are feasible. Thus $p_C\ge a_C$, $0\le X_C\le1$ and $\E X_C=a_C$.

For disjoint cycles $C,C'$, let $\ell(C,C')$ count their direct joining edges and the components of $\Gamma-(V(C)\cup V(C'))$ attached to both. Prescribed inner cycle words multiply zero-cut probabilities by separate inner factors. These factors cancel in the ratio \eqref{eq:ratio}. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
 \E(X_CX_{C'})=a_Ca_{C'}\,2^{\ell(C,C')-1}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The deleted cycles count as cyclic components}\label{sec:links}
By \eqref{eq:cubic}, at most $c$ components of $\Gamma-(V(C)\cup V(C'))$ meet $P$. Every other component $F_i$ lies in $\Gamma-P$. Its cut $d_i$ ends on the two cycles, and $\sum_id_i\le2L$. Set $t_0=\floor{y/2}$. At most $2L/t_0$ components have $d_i>t_0$. Among the remaining components, at most $O(t_02^{t_0}/\eps)$ are cyclic, by \eqref{eq:small-cuts} and \eqref{eq:cubic}.

If $F_i$ is a tree, it cannot meet $S$. Every packed cycle that meets $F_i$ lies wholly in it, because the packed cycle is disjoint from $C,C'$. Thus each such tree lies in the high-girth graph $J$. Cubic ambient degree gives $|F_i|=d_i-2$.

If the tree meets both cycles and $d_i\le t_0$, choose a path through it that joins the cycles. Choose this path with length at most $t_0$. Include direct joining edges as paths of length one. These paths have disjoint interiors and distinct endpoints. Partition each cycle into consecutive vertex blocks of size at most $\floor{D/4}$.

Two paths between the same block pair would form a cycle of length at most $2t_0+D/2<D$. Thus there are only $O((L/D)^2)$ such paths. Uniformly in the pair,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:links}
 \ell(C,C')\le c+O\Bigl(\bigl(L/D\bigr)^2+L/t_0+t_02^{t_0}/\eps\Bigr)=c+o(x).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Complete the second moment bound}\label{sec:second}
Call a disjoint pair exceptional when $\ell(C,C')\ge3$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:exceptional}, a disjoint packing of exceptional partners of $C$ has at most $|\delta C|\le L$ members. A maximal packing has union $U_C$ of size at most $L^2$. This union meets every exceptional partner. Equation \eqref{eq:walks} bounds the cycle weight through a fixed vertex by $O(L2^{-s})$. Thus \eqref{eq:pair} and \eqref{eq:links} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exceptional-sum}
 \sum_{C'\text{ exceptional to }C}\E(X_CX_{C'})\le a_C\,2^{c+o(x)}\,O\bigl(L^32^{-s}\bigr)=o(1)\,a_C,
\end{equation}
because $c\le x/100$ and $s=x/20+O(1)$.

Intersecting distinct cycles are incompatible. Nonexceptional disjoint pairs contribute at most $2a_Ca_{C'}$. Also, $\E X_C^2\le a_C$. Thus, with $\mu=\E Z=V$,
\[
 \E Z^2\le2\mu^2+(1+o(1))\mu.
\]

Since every $C$ lies in $\Gamma-P$, equations \eqref{eq:second-moment} and \eqref{eq:collisions} give
\[
 P_\Gamma(\Gamma-P)\le\Prob(Z=0)\le\frac12+\frac{2+o(1)}{\ln y}<\frac12+\frac{20}z,
\]
which contradicts \eqref{eq:cubic}. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:forest}.\qed

\section{Cycle lengths and the lower bound}\label{sec:recurrence}
%======================================================================
Let $\Phi(R)$ be the supremum of $(L-2)2^{-\beta(G)}$ over simple graphs of rank at least $R$ containing every cycle length $3,\ldots,L$. A cycle count gives $\Phi\le1$. Vertex identifications make realizers connected.

\begin{theorem}[Near-halving]\label{thm:recurrence}
For every sufficiently large integer $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 \Phi\bigl(2^{256R}\bigr)\le\Bigl(\frac12+\frac{20}{\log^{(2)}R}\Bigr)\Phi(R)+2^{2-R}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider a connected graph of rank $r\ge2^{256R}$ with every cycle length $3,\ldots,L$. If $L<2^{2R}+1$, then $(L-2)2^{-r}$ is below the error term. Otherwise, repeatedly add a cycle of the least missing length. Stop when the union $W$ first has rank $t\ge2R$.

At preceding rank $s<2R$, the added length is at most $2^s+2$. The improved bound is $\phi2^s+3$ when $s\ge R$, where $\phi=\Phi(R)$. Each step increases rank. Thus the process uses at most $2R$ cycles, and $\cc(W)\le2R$. With $m=|E(W)|$, we obtain
\[
 m\le2^{2R}+4R-1\le2^{3R},\qquad m+1\le\phi2^{2R}+2^R+5R,\qquad(m+1)2^{-t}\le\phi+2^{1-R}.
\]

Restriction to $M=E\setminus W$ has $2^{r-t}$ equally likely words. A mixed cycle requires an outside word that is a forest. Mixed cycles have at most $m+1$ lengths per outside word. Pure outside cycles contribute at most one length per word. Inside cycles contribute at most $m$ lengths. Thus
\[
 (L-2)2^{-r}\le(m+1)2^{-t}P_G(M)+2^{-t}+m2^{-r}.
\]

Theorem~\ref{thm:forest} applies because $m\le\sqrt r$ and $\cc(W)\le2R\le(\log r)/100$. Since $\log^{(3)}r\ge\log^{(2)}R$, the coefficient of $\phi$ is at most the coefficient in \eqref{eq:recurrence}. Also use $P_G(M)\le1$. The remaining terms satisfy
\[
 2^{1-R}+2^{-2R}+2^{3R-r}\le2^{2-R}
\]
for large $R$. This proves \eqref{eq:recurrence}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}[Lower bound]\label{cor:lower}
Every pancyclic graph on $n$ vertices has at least $n+\log n+\lstar n-O(1)$ edges.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose an integer $R_0$ large enough for Theorem~\ref{thm:recurrence}. Iterate $R_{j+1}=2^{256R_j}$. For $j\ge2$, the identity $\log^{(2)}R_j=8+256R_{j-2}$ gives
\[
 \sum_j\frac1{\log^{(2)}R_j}<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_j2^{j-R_j}<\infty,
\]
and \eqref{eq:recurrence} gives $\Phi(R_j)=O(2^{-j})$.

Also, $\lstar R_j=j+O(1)$. For the upper bound, compare with $U_{j+1}=2^{U_j}$. Prove $R_j\le\sqrt{U_j}$ by induction, with a large fixed $U_0$ as the initial value. The reverse bound follows from $R_{j+1}\ge2^{R_j}$. Monotonicity then gives
\[
 \Phi(r)=O\bigl(2^{-\lstar r}\bigr).
\]

A pancyclic $n$-vertex graph with $n+h$ edges has rank $r=h+1$. Thus $n-2=O(2^{r-\lstar r})$ and $n\le2^{r+1}$. These bounds give $\lstar n\le\lstar r+2$. Therefore $h\ge\log n+\lstar n-O(1)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The matching construction}\label{sec:upper}
%======================================================================
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:upper}
For every $n\ge3$ there is a pancyclic graph on $n$ vertices with at most $n+\floor{\log n}+\lstar n+1$ edges.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $3\le n\le6$, start with an $n$-cycle $v_0\cdots v_{n-1}$. The construction includes the chord $v_0v_2$ when $n\ge4$. It also includes $v_0v_3$ when $n=6$. These graphs satisfy the proposition.

For $n\ge7$, choose the largest $k$ with $2^k+k+1\le n$. On an $n$-cycle, choose an arc with successive segment lengths $2^i+1$, $0\le i<k$. Its segment endpoints are $v_0,\ldots,v_k$. Add the $k$ segment shortcuts and $v_0v_k$. Binary subset sums give the cycle lengths
\[
 [k+1,\,2^k+k]\qquad\text{and}\qquad[n-2^k+1,\,n].
\]

Maximality of $k$ gives $n\le2^{k+1}+k+1$. Thus at most one intermediate length is missing. If this length is missing, one additional chord supplies it.

Start at $t=k$. While $t>2$, add $v_0v_j$ for $j=\ceil{\log t}<t$. Its shortcuts give $[j+1,2^j+j]$. Since $2^j\ge t$, this extends the interval of available lengths down to $j+1$. Replace $t$ by $j$.

This process uses $\lstar k-1$ further chords. The construction adds at most
\[
 k+\lstar k+1\le\floor{\log n}+\lstar n+1
\]
edges in total.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:lower} and Proposition~\ref{prop:upper} prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
